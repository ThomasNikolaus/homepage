Teaching

How I teach

In my courses, I aim to give you a clear conceptual overview together with careful attention to definitions, proofs, and examples. I take students seriously. This means that while I ask you to engage with difficult and meaningful material, I also offer the conceptual orientation, guidance, and support needed to do so. Questions and discussion are always welcome and are, for me, among the most important aspects of a course. Lecture notes and problem sheets are available for almost all of my courses.

My courses range from foundational undergraduate mathematics to advanced topics close to current research. At the beginning of each course, I try to give you a map of the subject—its central questions, conceptual goals, and why the material is worth learning. As the course develops, I encourage you to explore examples, formulate questions of your own, and become increasingly independent in approaching unfamiliar mathematics.

Bachelor’s and master’s theses

Interested in a project?

I have supervised 14 master’s theses and currently supervise four more. I also regularly supervise bachelor’s theses, reading courses, and research projects in topology, higher algebra, algebraic K-theory, and related areas. Topics can be tailored to your background and interests.
