Homotopical Cartography

Charting structures and difficulty landscapes across mathematics using homotopical methods and AI tools.

A collaborative research format. Twice-weekly charting sessions explore mathematical structures, test routes with and without AI tools, and identify where the real difficulties lie.

Each thematic block concludes with a jointly written Exploration Chart, documenting the mathematics discovered, the routes taken, the obstacles encountered, and informative failures.

Together, these charts form The Atlas: an evolving, connected collection of mathematical explorations. We will develop ways to link and navigate this work, using visual or interactive maps where they prove useful.

A shared research process

In our charting sessions, we work in thematic blocks lasting one to three weeks, focused on questions, directions, or connections carefully chosen together to complement ongoing research.

Each block culminates in a jointly written Exploration Chart, documenting the mathematics discovered, routes explored, and understanding gained.

Each Exploration Chart discusses six connected aspects:

  1. What we explored

    The starting point, motivation, and territory investigated.

  2. What we learned

    The resulting mathematical understanding is the core of the Chart: precise statements, examples, counterexamples, and complete proofs where appropriate.

  3. What worked

    The productive ideas, examples, methods, and uses of AI tools that moved the exploration forward.

  4. What did not work

    Informative failures: false analogies, blocked approaches, systematic AI errors, refuted conjectures, or routes that generated output without insight.

  5. Difficulty landscape

    What was hard to discover, produce, verify, or understand? Where was human orientation essential, and which parts were routine for the tools used?

  6. Where this points next

    New questions, promising regions, unresolved obstacles, and directions worth a separate exploration block.
