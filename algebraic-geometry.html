Algebraic Geometry

This course develops the language of schemes and uses it to study geometric spaces defined by polynomial equations. The guiding principle is to move freely between algebraic structure and geometric intuition.

Course overview

From rings to schemes

This course continues the lecture Commutative Algebra. After a short introduction to category theory, we will develop the theory of schemes, including central properties of schemes and morphisms of schemes, as well as quasi-coherent and locally free sheaves.

The course will culminate in the construction of the Grassmannian, which parametrizes the d-dimensional linear subspaces of an n-dimensional vector space.

  1. 01Category theory
  2. 02Affine schemes and schemes
  3. 03Morphisms and their properties
  4. 04Quasi-coherent and locally free sheaves
  5. 05The Grassmannian

Motivation

What is the idea of algebraic geometry?

Algebraic geometry begins with systems of polynomial equations, but its aim is not merely to list their solutions. It studies the geometry carried by those solutions and uses that geometry to answer algebraic questions—and conversely.

Linear algebra

Systems of linear equations have solution sets that form vector subspaces.

Algebra

Polynomial equations encode extensions, arithmetic information, and relations in rings.

Algebraic geometry

Several polynomial equations define spaces whose algebraic and geometric structures illuminate one another.

Think of x2 + y2 = 1 as a circle—not merely as a set of pairs.

As in differential geometry, spaces arise locally as zero loci of functions. Here the functions are polynomial, and we deliberately allow singularities. The theory of schemes records the additional algebraic information needed to make this viewpoint work over general fields and rings.

Reading

Literature

Textbooks in algebraic geometry

  • S. Bosch, Algebraic Geometry and Commutative AlgebraSpringer, 2013. A self-contained treatment of commutative algebra and the theory of schemes.
  • D. Eisenbud and J. Harris, The Geometry of SchemesGraduate Texts in Mathematics 197, Springer, 2000. Detailed, illustrative, and rich in examples.
  • U. Görtz and T. Wedhorn, Algebraic Geometry I: SchemesVieweg+Teubner, 2010. A comprehensive introduction with many examples and substantial motivation.
  • U. Görtz and T. Wedhorn, Algebraic Geometry II: Cohomology of SchemesSpringer Spektrum, 2023. A comprehensive continuation devoted to cohomological methods.
  • R. Hartshorne, Algebraic GeometryGraduate Texts in Mathematics 52, Springer, 1977. The classical standard reference.
  • Q. Liu, Algebraic Geometry and Arithmetic CurvesOxford University Press, 2002. Particularly useful for the arithmetic side of the subject.

More on commutative algebra

  • M. F. Atiyah and I. G. Macdonald, Introduction to Commutative AlgebraAddison–Wesley, 1969. The standard concise introduction, with a clear exposition.
  • D. Eisenbud, Commutative Algebra with a View Toward Algebraic GeometryGraduate Texts in Mathematics 150, Springer, 1995. An extensive reference for the commutative algebra used in geometry.

Category theory

  • M. Brandenburg, Einführung in die KategorientheorieSpringer Spektrum, 2nd ed., 2017. A careful German-language introduction with many examples.
  • S. Mac Lane, Categories for the Working MathematicianGraduate Texts in Mathematics 5, Springer, 2nd ed., 1998. The standard general reference.
